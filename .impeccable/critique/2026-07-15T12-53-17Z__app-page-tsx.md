## Anti-Patterns Verdict

The page does not immediately read as AI-generated: its flat surfaces, real artefacts, restrained palette, and lack of decorative card clutter are appropriate. Its risk is a predictable section rhythm — heading, explanatory copy, ruled list — that makes the portfolio feel like a polished component inventory rather than an authored hiring argument.

The deterministic detector found no findings in `app/page.tsx`. Browser inspection and overlays were unavailable because no browser automation capability was exposed.

## Overall Impression

The visual system is credible and disciplined. The largest opportunity is narrative order: make inspectable, attributable proof the first thing after the hero, then let method and breadth explain the evidence.

## What's Working

- The blue action / teal orientation color roles and flat ruled system match an expert, engineering-minded portfolio.
- Course, video, presentation, and style-guide artefacts give the page real proof rather than invented performance claims.
- Source review found descriptive labels, visible focus styling, reduced-motion alternatives, responsive fallbacks, and sensible image priority.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Proof arrives too late and lacks a hiring decision path

**Why it matters:** A hiring manager must pass through leadership, approach, and teaching content before the strongest evidence. They cannot quickly answer what the candidate personally built.

**Fix:** Put two or three selected proof items directly after the hero. Each should state role, artefact, what it demonstrates, and an inspection link. Move method and capability inventory later.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Capability claims lack consistent attribution

**Why it matters:** Leadership, formats, technical foundation, and marketing voice can read as a CV claim stack when project rows do not say personal role, scope, context, and observable output.

**Fix:** Add factual attribution to every featured artefact and cut unsupported lists until each claim is paired with evidence.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Repeated section grammar creates scroll fatigue

**Why it matters:** Several sections repeat large heading, muted prose, and ruled list. Individually clean, together they make distinctions easy to skim past.

**Fix:** Consolidate overlapping self-description into one operating-system section; retain ruled lists for proof and vary the remaining sections by decision purpose.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] Unavailable proof weakens trust

**Why it matters:** “ссылка появится позже” and “фрагмент появится позже” ask a sceptical visitor to accept incomplete evidence.

**Fix:** Remove unfinished items from selected work, or mark them as private with a concrete alternative — outline, public excerpt, or request-access path.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

### [P3] Navigation is complete rather than recruiter-fast

**Why it matters:** Seven horizontally scrollable mobile items, while key destinations are absent, reads like a table of contents rather than a guided evaluation path.

**Fix:** Reduce navigation to Work, Approach, Expertise, Contact and keep one compact “предложить роль” action present.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan — hiring manager / first-time visitor

The headline is strong, but Jordan cannot immediately verify personal role, employer context, or selected-work relevance. Unavailable links in the first course group weaken the trust test.

### Riley — education-product leader

Programme and assessment capabilities are named, but the page does not pair each with an artefact and decision rationale. Frontend content proves more than educational-product leadership.

### Casey — engineering evaluator

Casey can inspect topics and style-guide work but lacks a fast route to code, curriculum depth, or explicit technical ownership. The technical-foundation list reads broad rather than verified.
